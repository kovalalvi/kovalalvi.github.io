Brain-computer interfaces in China: 12 companies, money and data

China approved the first implantable BCI, lets hospitals run implant studies on their own and raised over $1B in six months. 12 companies, their money and data.

AuthorAleksandr Kovalev
AffiliationUMH, Spain
PublishedNov 18, 2026
Typereview

Recently Bashar Badran from the Chinese startup Gestala visited us. He used to work at Neuralink, and now he runs clinical and science there. He told us how strong neurotech in China is right now. At AAAI I also talked a lot with a colleague from a BCI lab in Beijing. So I decided to figure out what is really going on there.

First thing I found: on March 13, 2026 China approved NEO by Neuracle, the world's first implantable brain-computer interface as a product. 9 days later it got an insurance billing code, 11 days later it was on public procurement lists (MIT Tech Review).

Then I found out that in China a hospital can start a human study of an implant by itself. Lab tests, materials and large-animal studies are still required (example), but the hospital reviews them, and since May 2026 the National Health Commission only gets a notification and can stop the study later (NHC). In the US, the FDA reviews all of this before the start.

And money. In the first half of 2026 alone, Chinese BCI raised ~$1.08B, 3x all of 2025 (36Kr). US BCI startups raised $932M from January to mid-November (New Market Pitch).

what: the first approved implant, NEO. For people with tetraplegia after a neck injury: close and open the hand.

  • Basically 8 contacts on top of the dura, nothing goes into the cortex. No battery, it works while a coil sits on the head. An external computer decodes and inflates a glove on the hand
  • One patient: F1 0.91 over 9 months at home (medRxiv). My first thought was that EEG has done the same for years. I found a similar patient: ~70% accuracy after a year of training (Rohm 2013). Different metrics, but the gap is clear
  • A kit costs $71k, 13 sold since July. No paper yet on the 32 patients from the trial

My thoughts

Time to move to China 😅

OK, seriously: China invests a lot in neurotech. There is both private and state money. The regulator makes it easier to test new technologies in humans, so you can work with a hospital directly. It may look like there are not many papers and details, but we can say the same about American companies. In my view, this is fine. It is hard to demand papers in top journals from startups.

On vision: Mindtrix alone raised more than 10x what ReVision and Phosphoenix raised together in 2026. And the Europeans have no human data at all, while Mindtrix has at least one test (on a sighted patient, during a different procedure).

China is quickly becoming the second neurotech center in the world, right after the US. My prediction: in 5–10 years China can take first place by the number of implanted neural devices.
